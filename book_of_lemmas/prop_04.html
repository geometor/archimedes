Proposition 4: Area of the Arbelos (Ἄρβηλος)¶

Proposition 4 (Area of the Arbelos). If AB be the diameter of a semicircle and N any point on AB, and if semicircles be described within the first semicircle and having AN, NB as diameters respectively, the figure included between the circumferences of the three semicircles is ‘what Archimedes called ἄρβηλος’ (shoemaker’s knife); and its area is equal to the circle on PN as diameter, where PN is perpendicular to AB and meets the original semicircle in P.

Proof: By Eucl. III. 31 and Eucl. VI. 8, , so . Since circles and semicircles are to one another as the squares on their diameters (Eucl. XII. 2), the outer semicircle on AB equals the sum of the two inner semicircles on AN, NB together with twice the semicircle (i.e. one whole circle) on PN. Subtracting the two inner semicircles leaves the arbelos equal to the circle on PN!
