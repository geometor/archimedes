On the Sphere and Cylinder II: Spherical Segments & Eutocius's Cubic Intersection
=================================================================================

.. folio:: Heath (1897), pp. 59–79 · Heiberg I, 194–225 & III, 130–177
   :page: 59

* **Book II, Proposition 2** (:cite:`archimedes:sphere-and-cylinder:ii-2`): Every spherical segment of height :eq:`h` in a sphere of radius :eq:`r` equals the cone having the same base as the segment and altitude :eq:`h \cdot \frac{3r - h}{2r - h}`, whence :eq:`V_{\text{seg}} = \frac{1}{3}\pi h^2 (3r - h)`.
* **Book II, Proposition 4**: *To cut a given sphere by a plane so that the volumes of the segments have to one another a given ratio* :eq:`m : n`. Analysis reduces the division of diameter :eq:`2r` to the **cubic proportion**:

  .. math::

     (a - x) : b = c^2 : x^2, \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad x^2(a - x) = b c^2.

  By the Doric synthesis and *diorismos* preserved by :person:`Eutocius of Ascalon <eutocius>` (:cite:`eutocius:in-archimedem`; Heath pp. 65–79), the root :eq:`x \in (0, a)` is constructed as the intersection of the **parabola** :eq:`x^2 = \frac{c^2}{k} y` with the **rectangular hyperbola** :eq:`(a - x) y = b k`, and the **maximum (*diorismos*)** of :eq:`x^2(a - x)` on :eq:`(0, a)` is attained uniquely at :eq:`x = \frac{2}{3}a` (namely :eq:`\frac{4}{27}a^3`), where the parabola and hyperbola touch one another.
