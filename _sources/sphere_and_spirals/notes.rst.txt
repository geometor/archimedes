Sphere and Cylinder, On Spirals & Conoids — Philological & Mathematical Notes
=============================================================================

1. Cicero and the Tomb of Archimedes at Syracuse (75 BC)
--------------------------------------------------------

In *Tusculan Disputations* V.23, Cicero records how, serving as quaestor in Sicily in 75 BC, he cleared away brambles outside the Agrigentine Gate of Syracuse and rediscovered the forgotten tomb of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` by recognizing on a small column the **sphere and circumscribed cylinder** of *Sphere and Cylinder* I.34 Corollary, accompanied by worn Greek iambic verses declaring that the cylinder's volume and surface stood in the sesquialter ratio :eq:`3 : 2` to the sphere. As :doc:`The Method, Prop. 2 <../the_method/prop_02>` reveals in Archimedes' own words, he discovered the volume :eq:`V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3` *first* by weighing slices on a virtual balance, and *then* conjectured :eq:`S = 4\pi r^2` by analogy with *Measurement of a Circle* Prop. 1 (:eq:`A = \frac{1}{2}Cr \leftrightarrow V = \frac{1}{3}Sr`)!

2. Eutocius's Recovery of Archimedes' Doric Cubic Manuscript (II.4)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

In *Sphere and Cylinder* II.4, Archimedes reduces cutting a sphere in a given ratio to dividing a line :eq:`a` at :eq:`x` so that :eq:`x^2(a - x) = bc^2`, adding *"an analysis and synthesis of both problems will be given at the end."* For centuries the supplement seemed lost, inspiring Dionysodorus and Diocles to invent independent conic solutions. As :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` (:cite:`eutocius:in-archimedem`, Heiberg Vol. III, pp. 130–132; :cite:`heath:1897`, p. xxxvi) recounts, he stumbled upon a worn old book (*ἔν τινι παλαιῷ βιβλίῳ*) written in **Syracusan Doric dialect** and using Archimedes' archaic pre-Apollonian conic names (*ὀρθογωνίου κώνου τομή* for parabola, *ἀμβλυγωνίου κώνου τομή* for hyperbola), proving beyond doubt that it was Archimedes' own missing *diorismos*!

3. GEOMETOR Cross-Corpus Concordance
------------------------------------

* **Euclid (`geometor/euclid`)** (:cite:`geometor:euclid`): Covers four required cross-corpus citations in Heath's *Euclid*:
  - ``archimedes:sphere-and-cylinder`` (Euclid I.Def.4 straight line, X.1 exhaustion, XII.18 spheres);
  - ``archimedes:sphere-and-cylinder:ii-2`` (Euclid XII commentary on spherical segments);
  - ``archimedes:conoids-and-spheroids`` (Euclid XII.2 affine scaling of circles to ellipses & XII.10 cones);
  - ``eutocius:in-archimedem`` (Eutocius's catalogue of 12 cube-duplication constructions & II.4 cubic).
* **Proclus (`geometor/proclus`)** (:cite:`proclus:in-euclidem`): At *Friedlein* p. 110, :person:`Proclus <proclus>` contrasts Euclid's definition of the straight line (*ἥτις ἐξ ἴσου τοῖς ἐφ᾽ ἑαυτῆς σημείοις κεῖται*) with Archimedes' metric postulate (*ἐλαχίστη τῶν τὰ αὐτὰ πέρατα ἐχουσῶν*, Assump. 1), arguing that minimum length is an accidental quantitative symptom rather than the ontological essence of straightness.
* **Pappus (`geometor/pappus`)** (:cite:`pappus:synagoge:iv`): In *Synagoge* IV.21–25, :person:`Pappus <pappus>` simplifies Archimedes' spiral quadrature and discusses why Archimedes' :term:`neusis <neusis>` tangents in *On Spirals* Props. 5–9 employ solid/marked-straightedge means where conic/planar reductions exist.
