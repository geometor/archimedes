Semi-Regular Solids, *Stomachion* & *Sand-Reckoner* — Philological & Mathematical Notes
=======================================================================================

1. Archimedes → Pappus → Pacioli & Leonardo → Kepler
----------------------------------------------------

In *Synagoge* V.19 (:cite:`pappus:synagoge:v`), :person:`Pappus <pappus>` lists the thirteen solids in increasing order of complexity within the tetrahedral/cubic and icosahedral families (`8, 14, 14, 14, 26, 26, 38, 32, 32, 32, 62, 62, 92` faces) and computes their vertex and edge numbers by face-incidence counting (:eq:`2E = \sum k F_k`). During the Renaissance, Piero della Francesca and :person:`Luca Pacioli <pacioli>` (:cite:`geometor:pacioli`, *De Divina Proportione*, Venice 1509, with woodcuts after Leonardo da Vinci's *corpora vacua* skeletal drawings) rediscovered the **truncated** and **elevated** (*abscisi / elevati*) members directly from the five Platonic solids of Euclid Book XIII, before :person:`Johannes Kepler <kepler>` (*Harmonices Mundi*, Linz 1619) restored all thirteen—including the enantiomorphic **snub cube** and **snub dodecahedron**—by systematic angular-defect analysis (:eq:`\sum \alpha_i < 2\pi` at every convex vertex, :just:`Eucl. XI. 21`).

2. GEOMETOR Cross-Corpus Concordance
------------------------------------

* **Pacioli (`geometor/pacioli`) & Divine (`geometor/divine`)** (:cite:`geometor:pacioli`): Solids **VIII–XII** (`Icosidodecahedron`, `Truncated Icosahedron`, `Truncated Dodecahedron`, `Rhombicosidodecahedron`, `Truncated Icosidodecahedron`) have exact vertex coordinates belonging purely to the **golden quadratic field** :eq:`\mathbb{Q}(\phi)` (:eq:`\phi^2 = \phi + 1`), uniting Archimedes' lost polyhedral treatise directly with Euclid XIII.16–17 and Pacioli's *Divine Proportion*.
* **Pappus (`geometor/pappus`)** (:cite:`geometor:pappus`, :cite:`pappus:synagoge:v`): Bridges Pappus Book V (isoperimetric and polyhedral comparison) with Archimedes' solid geometry.
* **Biographical & Astronomical Witness**: *The Sand-Reckoner* preserves two unique historical testimonies found nowhere else in antiquity: (1) the name and astronomical work of Archimedes' father :person:`Phidias <phidias>`, and (2) the verbatim heliocentric hypotheses of :person:`Aristarchus of Samos <aristarchus>`—cited eighteen centuries later in Copernicus's manuscript of *De revolutionibus*!
