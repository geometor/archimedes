On the Equilibrium of Planes — Analytical Summary
=================================================

*Scope*: **Heiberg Vol. II, pp. 142–241** · **Heath (1897), pp. 189–220** · *Book I (7 Postulates, Props. 1–15) & Book II (Props. 1–10)*

Deductive Architecture of Rational Mechanics
--------------------------------------------

*On the Equilibrium of Planes* (*Ἐπιπέδων ἰσορροπιῶν*) is the founding treatise of **theoretical mechanics** and **barycentric geometry**. Without appealing to dynamic virtual velocities (as in the Peripatetic *Mechanica*), :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` axiomatizes static :term:`equilibrium <isorropia>` from pure symmetry, monotonicity, convexity, and Eudoxean proportion:

1. **Symmetry to Commensurable Lever (I.Post.1–7 → I.1–6)**: By splitting two commensurable weights :g:`A`, :g:`B` into :eq:`2p` and :eq:`2q` equal unit weights distributed uniformly along a beam of total length :eq:`2 \cdot DE`, Archimedes reduces unequal reciprocal leverage :eq:`A : B = DC : CE` directly to the midpoint symmetry of a single homogeneous rod!
2. **Eudoxean Extension to Irrationals (I.7)**: Using :just:`Euclid V. Def. 4` (:cite:`euclid:elements`), Archimedes extends the :term:`Law of the Lever <antipeponthos>` to incommensurable ratios (:eq:`\sqrt{2}`, :eq:`\phi`, etc.) via rational insertion.
3. **Rectilinear Barycenters (I.8–15)**: Derives in strict sequence the remainder theorem (I.8), the parallelogram diagonal intersection (I.9–10), the **triangle centroid** ``X(2)`` as concurrency of the medians (I.13–14), and the trapezium centroid formula (I.15).
4. **Self-Similar Parabolic Centroids (II.1–10)**: Exploiting the affine self-similarity of parabolic segments (II.7), Book II solves for the parabolic centroid :eq:`AG : GO = 3 : 2` algebraically without needing a fresh infinite summation of moments—a breathtaking anticipation of functional equations and renormalization!
