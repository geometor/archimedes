On the Equilibrium of Planes — Philological & Mathematical Notes
================================================================

1. Preservation of Syracusan Doric Dialect
------------------------------------------

Unlike *Measurement of a Circle* and *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, *On the Equilibrium of Planes* escaped the sixth-century Atticizing recension of :person:`Isidore of Miletus <isidore-of-miletus>` and preserves :person:`Archimedes' <archimedes>` authentic **Syracusan Doric Greek** (:cite:`heiberg:1881`, Vol. II, pp. 142–241). For instance, the enunciation of Book I, Prop. 9 (Heiberg II, 162, verified from 300 DPI scan) reads:

.. epigraph::

   *Παντὸς παραλληλογράμμου τὸ κέντρον τοῦ βάρεός ἐστιν ἐπὶ τᾶς εὐθείας τᾶς ἐπιζευγνυούσας τὰς διχοτομίας τᾶν κατ᾽ ἐναντίον τοῦ παραλληλογράμμου πλευρᾶν.*

Notice the Doric genitive singular *τᾶς εὐθείας* (Attic *τῆς εὐθείας*) and genitive plural *τᾶν ... πλευρᾶν* (Attic *τῶν ... πλευρῶν*), matching the Doric forms in *On Spirals*, *The Sand-Reckoner*, and *Quadrature of the Parabola*.

2. Mach's Critique of Book I, Prop. 6 & Archimedes' Implicit Postulate
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Ernst Mach (*Die Mechanik in ihrer Entwicklung*, 1883) famously questioned whether replacing two weights :g:`A`, :g:`B` at :g:`E`, :g:`D` by spread-out arrays of unit weights centered at :g:`E` and :g:`D` tacitly assumes the very lever law being proved. As :person:`Heath <heath>` (:cite:`heath:1897`, pp. 192–193) and Giovanni Vailati demonstrated, Archimedes' derivation is **strictly non-circular** once one recognizes **Postulate 6** (*magnitudes equal to balancing magnitudes balance at the same distances*) together with the definition of :term:`centre of gravity <kentron-barous>` established in Archimedes' lost preliminary tract *On Balances* (*Περὶ ζυγῶν*, cited by :person:`Pappus <pappus>` in :cite:`pappus:synagoge:viii`): replacing a subsystem of equal weights by an equal composite weight located at its midpoint centre of gravity relies solely on *equal-arm symmetry* (:just:`Prop. 4–5`), never on unequal-arm moments!

3. GEOMETOR Cross-Corpus Concordance
------------------------------------

* **Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers (`geometor/etc`)** (:cite:`geometor:etc`): **Book I, Propositions 13–14** constitute the earliest rigorous proof in mathematical history that the three medians of a triangle are concurrent—defining Kimberling's **Centroid `X(2)`** with homogeneous barycentric coordinates :eq:`(1 : 1 : 1)` and trilinear coordinates :eq:`(bc : ca : ab)`. Indeed, Möbius's entire 1827 *barycentrischer Calcul* is the direct algebraic descendant of Archimedes' Book I.
* **Euclid (`geometor/euclid`)** (:cite:`geometor:euclid`): Heath cites :cite:`archimedes:equilibrium-of-planes:i-7` in his commentary on Euclid Book V as the paradigm of applying Eudoxean proportion theory to continuous physical magnitudes.
* **The Method (`Unit 6`)**: Every mechanical discovery in :doc:`Quadrature of the Parabola <../quadrature_of_the_parabola/index>` and :doc:`The Method <../the_method/prop_02>` rests directly upon **Book I, Props. 6–8 & 14** and **Book II, Prop. 8**.
