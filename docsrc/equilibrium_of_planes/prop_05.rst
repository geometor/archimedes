Book I, Proposition 5: Centre of Gravity of Equally Spaced Equal Magnitudes
===========================================================================

.. folio:: Heath (1897), pp. 190–192 · Heiberg II, 144–153
   :page: 191

.. enunciation::

   **Proposition 5 (with Corollaries 1–2).** If three equal magnitudes have their :term:`centres of gravity <kentron-barous>` on a straight line at equal distances, the centre of gravity of the system will coincide with that of the middle magnitude. (*Corollaries 1–2*: The same holds for any odd number of equally spaced equal magnitudes; for any even number, it lies at the midpoint between the two middle magnitudes.)

*Proof*: By :just:`Prop. 4`, the outer pair balances at the centre of the middle magnitude, so the combined system has its centre of gravity there (and inductively by pairing symmetric endpoints for any finite number).

.. qed::
