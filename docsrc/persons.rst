.. _works-persons:

People
======

.. rubric:: Biographical Register of Ancient Geometers, Translators & Commentators

Chronological register of geometers, astronomers, Syracusan patrons, commentators,
and philologists linked via ``:person:`` across the Archimedean corpus.

Authors, Translators & Editors
------------------------------

.. _person-archimedes:

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἀρχιμήδης ὁ Συρακούσιος*
* **Role**: Supreme geometer and founder of mathematical mechanics & hydrostatics
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Passim (Heath 1897 pp. 1–326; Heath 1912 pp. 1–51; Heiberg Vols. I–III)

Established rigorous rational bounds for π (3 10/71 < π < 3 1/7), founded theoretical statics and barycentric geometry (center of gravity X(2) of the triangle and parabolic segment), squared the parabola and spiral, cubed the sphere (2/3 of the circumscribed cylinder, engraved on his tomb), classified the 13 semi-regular polyhedra, and bequeathed his heuristic mechanical slice method to Eratosthenes.

.. _person-heath:

Sir Thomas Little Heath (1861–1940)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Thomas Little Heath*
* **Role**: Editor & translator of The Works of Archimedes (1897) and The Method (1912)
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Passim (Heath 1897 & 1912)

Produced the monumental Cambridge editions of Archimedes (1897, Supplement 1912), Apollonius (1896), and Euclid's Elements (1908, geometor/euclid), combining philological fidelity to Heiberg with crystalline geometric clarity.

.. _person-heiberg:

Johan Ludvig Heiberg (1854–1928)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Iohannes Ludovicus Heiberg*
* **Role**: Critical Teubner editor & discoverer of the Archimedes Palimpsest (1906)
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Heath 1897 Pref.; Heath 1912 Introductory Note (pp. 5–11)

Established the definitive critical Greek edition Archimedis Opera Omnia cum commentariis Eutocii (Teubner, 1st ed. 1880–1881; 2nd ed. 1910–1915) and in 1906 deciphered Codex C (the Constantinople Palimpsest), recovering the lost Greek text of The Method, Stomachion, and On Floating Bodies.

Cited Geometers, Philosophers & Scholars
----------------------------------------

.. _person-democritus:

Democritus of Abdera (c. 460–370 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Δημόκριτος ὁ Ἀβδηρίτης*
* **Role**: Atomist philosopher & pioneer of infinitesimal volume discovery
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Method Preface (Heath 1912, p. 13; Heiberg II, 430)

Explicitly credited by Archimedes in the Preface to The Method with first discovering—though without rigorous geometric demonstration—that the cone is one-third of the cylinder and the pyramid one-third of the prism on the same base and equal height.

.. _person-eudoxus:

Eudoxus of Cnidus (c. 390–337 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Εὔδοξος ὁ Κνίδιος*
* **Role**: Founder of the general theory of proportions & method of exhaustion
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sphere & Cyl. I Pref. (p. 2); Quadrature Pref. (p. 234); Method Pref. (1912, p. 13)

Formulated the Lemma of Continuity (Euclid V.Def.4, X.1; Archimedes Sphere & Cylinder I Assump. 5) and gave the first rigorous geometric proofs by exhaustion that a cone is one-third of a cylinder and a pyramid one-third of a prism, praised in Archimedes' prefaces to Sphere and Cylinder I and The Method.

.. _person-aristaeus:

Aristaeus the Elder (fl. c. 330 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἀρισταῖος ὁ Πρεσβύτερος*
* **Role**: Author of Solid Loci (Five Books on Conic Sections)
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Quadrature of the Parabola Props. 1–3 (pp. 235–236); Conoids & Spheroids Prop. 3

Pre-Apollonian geometer whose symptomata of the three sections of right-angled, acute-angled, and obtuse-angled cones form the background of Archimedes' citations to the 'Elements of Conics' (τὰ κωνικὰ στοιχεῖα).

.. _person-euclid:

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Εὐκλείδης*
* **Role**: Author of the Elements & Conics
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sphere & Cyl. I.2 (p. 5), I.6 (p. 9); Quadrature Prop. 3 (p. 236)

Cited by name in On the Sphere and Cylinder I.2 ('by the second proposition of the first book of Euclid') and I.6, and throughout Archimedes' demonstrations via the Elements of Geometry and Elements of Conics.

.. _person-phidias:

Phidias of Syracuse (fl. c. 290 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Φειδίας*
* **Role**: Astronomer & father of Archimedes
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sand-Reckoner I.9 (Heath 1897, p. 223; Heiberg II, 220)

Named by Archimedes in The Sand-Reckoner ('Phidias my father', Φειδία δὲ τοῦ ἁμοῦ πατρός) as having estimated the diameter of the sun to be twelve times that of the moon.

.. _person-aristarchus:

Aristarchus of Samos (c. 310–230 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἀρίσταρχος ὁ Σάμιος*
* **Role**: Mathematician & founder of ancient heliocentric astronomy
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sand-Reckoner I.4–7 (Heath 1897, pp. 221–223; Heiberg II, 218)

Author of hypotheses in which the fixed stars and sun remain unmoved while the earth revolves around the sun in a circle whose ratio to the sphere of fixed stars Archimedes analyzes and enlarges in The Sand-Reckoner (Arenarius I.4–7).

.. _person-hiero-ii:

King Hiero II of Syracuse (c. 308–215 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἱέρων Β΄*
* **Role**: Ruler of Syracuse & kinsman-patron of Archimedes
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sand-Reckoner I.1 (p. 221); On Floating Bodies I–II (pp. 253–300)

Commissioned the votive golden crown whose specific-gravity test inspired On Floating Bodies, witnessed Archimedes launch the giant ship Syracusia via compound pulleys, and prompted Archimedes' defensive engines that held off Marcellus's Roman fleet.

.. _person-conon:

Conon of Samos (c. 280–220 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Κόνων ὁ Σάμιος*
* **Role**: Court astronomer at Alexandria & closest mathematical confidant of Archimedes
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sphere & Cyl. I Pref. (p. 1); Spirals Pref. (p. 151); Quadrature Pref. (p. 233)

Celebrated with deep affection by Archimedes for his extraordinary geometric insight; Archimedes sent his theorems on spirals, spheres, and conoids first to Conon, mourning his premature death in the prefaces to Quadrature of the Parabola, Sphere and Cylinder I, and On Spirals.

.. _person-eratosthenes:

Eratosthenes of Cyrene (c. 276–194 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἐρατοσθένης ὁ Κυρηναῖος*
* **Role**: Chief Librarian at Alexandria, geometer & addressee of The Method
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Method Letter & Props. 1–15 (Heath 1912, pp. 12–51); Cattle Problem (p. 319)

Honored by Archimedes as an earnest scholar of philosophy and mathematics; recipient of The Method of Mechanical Theorems—in which Archimedes unveiled his private heuristic balance for discovering areas and volumes—and of the Cattle Problem.

.. _person-gelon:

King Gelon II of Syracuse (c. 266–216 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Γέλων*
* **Role**: Co-regent of Syracuse & addressee of The Sand-Reckoner
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sand-Reckoner I.1–IV (Heath 1897, pp. 221–232)

Son of Hiero II; addressed directly ('King Gelon', βασιλεῦ Γέλων) throughout The Sand-Reckoner, where Archimedes demonstrates his myriad-octad number system capable of exceeding the grains of sand filling Aristarchus's cosmos.

.. _person-dositheus:

Dositheus of Pelusium (fl. c. 230 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Δωσίθεος ὁ Πηλουσιώτης*
* **Role**: Alexandrian geometer, pupil of Conon & recipient of five Archimedean treatises
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sphere & Cyl. I–II (pp. 1, 57); Conoids (p. 99); Spirals (p. 151); Quadrature (p. 233)

After Conon's death, Archimedes addressed five monumental works—Quadrature of the Parabola, On the Sphere and Cylinder Books I and II, On Conoids and Spheroids, and On Spirals—to Dositheus in Alexandria.

.. _person-zeuxippus:

Zeuxippus (fl. c. 230 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ζεύξιππος*
* **Role**: Recipient of Archimedes' lost arithmetical treatise Principia
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sand-Reckoner I.8, III.1 (Heath 1897, pp. 222, 227)

Mentioned in The Sand-Reckoner as the recipient of Archimedes' earlier work Principles (Ἀρχαί) on the naming of arbitrarily large numbers beyond the myriad.

.. _person-apollonius:

Apollonius of Perga (c. 240–190 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἀπολλώνιος ὁ Περγαῖος*
* **Role**: The 'Great Geometer', author of Conics & Okytokion
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Heath 1897 Introduction Ch. IV & Measurement Note (pp. lxxvii, 93)

Codified the terminology of parabola, ellipse, and hyperbola, composed the Okytokion ('Quick-Delivery', attested by Eutocius) calculating tighter bounds on π than 22/7, and rivaled Archimedes' myriad-system in his tetrads.

.. _person-heron:

Heron of Alexandria (fl. c. 60 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἥρων ὁ Ἀλεξανδρεύς*
* **Role**: Geometer & engineer preserving citations to The Method and Mechanics
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Heath 1912 Intro (pp. 6–8); Wildberger Quadrea / Archimedes' Function

In his Metrica (rediscovered 1896), Heron explicitly cited Archimedes' Method on the cylindrical ungula and bicylinder before Heiberg found the Palimpsest, and transmitted the triangle area formula attributed by Arabic tradition (al-Bīrūnī) to Archimedes.

.. _person-pappus:

Pappus of Alexandria (c. 290–350 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Πάππος ὁ Ἀλεξανδρεύς*
* **Role**: Author of the Mathematical Collection (Synagoge), preserver of the 13 Archimedean Solids
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Pappus Synagoge IV.14–25, V.19, VIII; Heath 1897 Ch. V & Lemmas Prop. 6

Preserved the sole complete ancient account of Archimedes' Thirteen Semi-Regular Polyhedra in Synagoge Book V (Prop. 19), investigated the 'ancient proposition' of the infinite arbelos circle chain in Book IV, and extended Archimedes' spiral, neusis, and centroid theorems in Books IV and VIII.

.. _person-proclus:

Proclus Diadochus (412–485 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Πρόκλος ὁ Διάδοχος*
* **Role**: Neoplatonic scholarch & historian of Greek geometry
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sphere & Cyl. I Assump. 1 (p. 3); Proclus In Euclidem 63, 110

Cited Archimedes' On the Sphere and Cylinder on the definition of the straight line as the shortest distance between two points (Friedlein 110) and recounted King Hiero's admiration of Archimedes' ships and crown (Friedlein 63).

.. _person-eutocius:

Eutocius of Ascalon (c. 480–540 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Εὐτόκιος ὁ Ἀσκαλωνίτης*
* **Role**: Author of the surviving Greek Commentaries on Archimedes
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Sphere & Cyl. II.1, II.4 (pp. 62–79); Measurement Prop. 3; Heiberg Vol. III

Composed indispensable Greek commentaries on Sphere and Cylinder Books I–II (preserving twelve ancient solutions to cube duplication and Archimedes' missing cubic lemma II.4!), Measurement of a Circle (supplying full arithmetic verification of √3 bounds), and Equilibrium of Planes.

.. _person-isidore-of-miletus:

Isidore of Miletus (c. 442–537 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ἰσίδωρος ὁ Μιλήσιος*
* **Role**: Architect of Hagia Sophia & editor of Archimedes' Byzantine recension
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Heath 1897 Introduction Ch. II (pp. xxiii–xxv)

Supervised the sixth-century Constantinopolitan study of Archimedes and Eutocius's commentaries, normalizing parts of Archimedes' Syracusan Doric dialect in Sphere and Cylinder and Measurement of a Circle.

.. _person-leo-the-mathematician:

Leo the Mathematician (c. 790–869 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Λέων ὁ Μαθηματικός*
* **Role**: Byzantine polymath & compiler of Archetype Codex A
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Heath 1897 Introduction Ch. II (p. xxv); Heath 1912 Intro

Gathered surviving Greek manuscripts of Archimedes in ninth-century Constantinople into the minuscule archetype Codex A (lost after 1564, ancestor of all Renaissance Greek copies), initiating the Byzantine mathematical revival alongside Codex C (the Palimpsest).

.. _person-thabit-ibn-qurra:

Thābit ibn Qurra (836–901 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Θάβιτ ἰμπν Κούρρα (ثابت بن قرة)*
* **Role**: Sabian mathematician & Arabic translator of Archimedes' Book of Lemmas
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Book of Lemmas Props. 1–15 (Heath 1897, pp. 301–318)

Working in Baghdad's House of Wisdom tradition, translated Archimedes' Sphere and Cylinder, Measurement of a Circle, and the fifteen propositions of the Book of Lemmas (Kitāb al-Maʾkhūdhāt / Liber Assumptorum), preserving the Arbelos, Twin Circles, Neusis Trisection, and Salinon.

.. _person-moerbeke:

William of Moerbeke (c. 1215–1286)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Guillelmus de Moerbeka*
* **Role**: Dominican translator of Archimedes Codices A and B at Viterbo (1269)
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Heath 1897 Introduction Ch. II (pp. xxv–xxviii) & Floating Bodies (p. 253)

Produced a literal word-for-word Latin translation in 1269 at the Papal court of Viterbo (Vatican Ottob. lat. 1850) from Greek Codices A and B, serving as the primary textual witness for On Floating Bodies prior to the 1906 Palimpsest discovery.

.. _person-pacioli:

Luca Pacioli (c. 1447–1517)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Fra Luca Bartolomeo de Pacioli*
* **Role**: Franciscan geometer & author of De Divina Proportione (1509)
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Unit 7: Thirteen Archimedean Semi-Regular Solids Concordance

Reconstructed the truncated and semi-regular 'elevated/abscised' Archimedean polyhedra from golden-ratio and Platonic hulls in De Divina Proportione (illustrated by Leonardo da Vinci), bridging Archimedes and Pappus to Renaissance geometry (geometor/pacioli).

.. _person-kepler:

Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Ioannes Keplerus*
* **Role**: Astronomer & geometer who re-proved all 13 Archimedean Solids
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Unit 7: Thirteen Archimedean Semi-Regular Solids Concordance

In Harmonices Mundi Book II (1619), independently reconstructed, proved the completeness of, and assigned the modern Latin names (cuboctahedron, icosidodecahedron, snub cube, snub dodecahedron, etc.) to all thirteen Archimedean semi-regular solids attested by Pappus.

.. _person-borelli:

Giovanni Alfonso Borelli & Abraham Ecchellensis (1608–1679 (ed. 1661))
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Original Name / Form**: *Iohannes Alphonsus Borellius*
* **Role**: Editors of the 1661 Florence editio princeps of Liber Assumptorum
* **Archimedean Witness & Loci**: Book of Lemmas Intro (Heath 1897, pp. xxxii, 301)

Translated a 10th-century Arabic manuscript of Thābit ibn Qurra's recension (with al-Nasawī's commentary) at the Medici Library in Florence, publishing Archimedes' Liber Assumptorum in Latin in 1661.
