The Method of Mechanical Theorems — Philological & Mathematical Notes
=====================================================================

1. From Lost Work (1897) to Palimpsest Recovery (1906–1912)
-----------------------------------------------------------

Comparing :cite:`heath:1897` with :cite:`heath:1912` captures one of the greatest dramatic arcs in the history of scholarship:

* In **1897** (:cite:`heath:1897`, p. xxxviii, verified from 300 DPI scan), :person:`Heath <heath>` could list *The Method* (*Ἐφόδιον*) only under **Lost Works**, known solely from a single sentence in Suidas stating that Theodosius wrote a commentary upon it!
* Just months later (1899/1903), Hermann Schöne published the newly discovered Constantinople codex of :person:`Heron's <heron>` *Metrica* (:cite:`heron:metrica`), which quoted verbatim the two *Method* theorems on the :term:`cylindrical ungula <ungula>` and :term:`bicylinder <bicylinder>`!
* In **1906**, alerted by Papadopoulos-Kerameus's catalogue of the Metochion of the Holy Sepulchre in Constantinople, :person:`Johan Ludvig Heiberg <heiberg>` inspected **Codex C**—a thirteenth-century Euchologion prayer-book overwritten atop a tenth-century mathematical parchment—and deciphered *The Method* (:cite:`heiberg:1907`), prompting Heath's **1912 Cambridge Supplement** (:cite:`heath:1912`). (Leaf gaps at the very end of Prop. 15 were restored independently by Zeuthen and Heiberg from the Preface and Heron's citation.)

2. Democritus's Dilemma and Noetic Epistemology
-----------------------------------------------

Archimedes' tribute to :person:`Democritus <democritus>` and :person:`Eudoxus <eudoxus>` in the Preface illuminates Plutarch's report (*De communibus notitiis* 39) of Democritus's atomic cone paradox: if a cone is sliced parallel to its base, are adjacent circular sections *equal* (making the cone a cylinder) or *unequal* (making the cone stepped)? By separating heuristic slice-weighing (*ἔνδειξις*) from finite-step Eudoxean :term:`exhaustion <exhaustion>` (*ἀπόδειξις*), Archimedes reaps the full creative power of Cavalieri indivisibles without sacrificing Aristotelian-Euclidean continuum rigor—resonating deeply with ``geometor/aristotle`` and ``geometor/hoenen`` on geometric intuition (*φαντασία*) versus demonstration.

3. GEOMETOR Cross-Corpus Concordance
------------------------------------

* **Euclid (`geometor/euclid`)** (:cite:`geometor:euclid`): Directly satisfies both ``heath:method-of-archimedes`` and ``archimedes:methodus`` cited in Heath's *Euclid* Vol. III (Introduction to Book XII on Democritus and Eudoxus's cone/pyramid proofs, Eucl. XII.7 & XII.10).
* **Curved-to-Rectilinear Exact Cubatures**: Notice that both **Prop. 14** (:eq:`V_{\text{ungula}} = \frac{1}{6} V_{\text{prism}}`) and **Prop. 15** (:eq:`V_{\text{bicylinder}} = \frac{2}{3} V_{\text{cube}}`) achieve rational cubatures of curved solids *free of* :eq:`\pi`, prefiguring Hippocrates' lunes in 3D and Steinmetz/Zu Chongzhi's spherical-square cross-sections!
