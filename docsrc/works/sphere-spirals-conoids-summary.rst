Sphere and Cylinder, On Spirals & Conoids — Analytical Summary
==============================================================

*Scope*: **Heiberg Vol. I, pp. 1–255, 272–499; Vol. II, pp. 1–141** · **Heath (1897), pp. 1–90, 99–188**

Master Synthesis of Curved Surfaces, Solids & Kinematic Loci
------------------------------------------------------------

Across these three treatises addressed to :person:`Dositheus <dositheus>`, :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` builds the complete pre-Newtonian integral and differential calculus of circles, spheres, quadrics of revolution, and polar spirals:

1. **Convexity & Arc/Surface Length Foundations (*Sphere & Cylinder* I, Assump. 1–5)**: Because neither Euclid nor Eudoxus possessed an axiom comparing curved arc lengths or curved surface areas against rectilinear bounds, Archimedes formulates :term:`ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ κοῖλα <concave-same-way>` (convexity) so that nested convex perimeters and surfaces obey strict monotonicity.
2. **Discrete Power Sums as Definite Integrals**:
   - Linear sum :eq:`\sum k \sim \frac{1}{2}n^2 \implies` **Paraboloid segment** :eq:`= \frac{1}{2}` cylinder :eq:`= \frac{3}{2}` cone (*Conoids* Props. 19–22).
   - Quadratic sum :eq:`\sum k^2 \sim \frac{1}{3}n^3 \implies` **Spiral sector** :eq:`= \frac{1}{3}` circle (*On Spirals* Props. 10, 24), **Hyperboloid** (*Conoids* Props. 25–26), and **Spheroid** (*Conoids* Props. 27–32).
3. **Differential Tangent Construction (*On Spirals* Props. 18–20)**: First rigorous tangent to a non-conic mechanical curve in mathematical history, yielding :eq:`OT = 2\pi r_1`.
