Prefatory Epistle to Dositheus & Archimedes' Lemma
==================================================

.. folio:: Heath (1897), pp. 233–235 · Heiberg II, 294–299
   :page: 233

“**ARCHIMEDES TO DOSITHEUS GREETING.**

When I heard that :person:`Conon <conon>`, who was my friend in his lifetime, was dead, but that you were acquainted with Conon and withal versed in geometry, while I grieved greatlily for the loss not only of a friend but of an admirable mathematician, I set myself the task of communicating to you, as I had intended to send to Conon, a certain geometrical theorem which had not been investigated before but has now been investigated by me, and which I first discovered by means of mechanics and then exhibited by means of geometry.”

Earlier geometers attempted in vain to square the circle and segments of the hyperbola or ellipse, whereas no one hitherto has sought to square the segment bounded by a straight line and a **section of a right-angled cone** (:term:`parabolic segment <tmema-paraboles>`). I have now proved that every such segment is **four-thirds** of the triangle having the same base and equal height; and for the demonstration of this theorem I assume the following lemma (:term:`Archimedes' Lemma <archimedes-lemma>`, cf. :cite:`hilbert:1899`):

.. enunciation::

   **Lemma.** *The excess by which the greater of (two) unequal areas exceeds the less can, by being added to itself, be made to exceed any given finite area.*

Earlier geometers (:person:`Eudoxus of Cnidus <eudoxus>`) also employed this very lemma to prove that circles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of their diameters (:just:`Eucl. XII. 2`), spheres in the triplicate ratio of their diameters (:just:`Eucl. XII. 18`), every pyramid one-third of the prism on the same base and equal altitude (:just:`Eucl. XII. 7`), and every cone one-third of the cylinder (:just:`Eucl. XII. 10`).
