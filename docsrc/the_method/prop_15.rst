Proposition 15: Mechanical & Geometric Cubature of the Bicylinder
=================================================================

.. folio:: Heath (1912), pp. 21–51 · Heiberg II², 446–507
   :page: 48

.. enunciation::

   **Proposition 15 (The Archimedean Bicylinder).** If two cylinders of equal diameter :eq:`2r` be inscribed at right angles to one another within a cube of edge :eq:`2r`, the solid common to both cylinders (:term:`bicylinder <bicylinder>` / groin vault) is equal to **two-thirds of the cube** (:eq:`V = \frac{16}{3} r^3`).

Slicing the two orthogonal cylinders by planes parallel to both axes produces **square cross-sections** of area :eq:`4(r^2 - x^2)`—identical in ratio at every height :eq:`x` to the circular cross-sections :eq:`\pi(r^2 - x^2)` of the inscribed sphere! Hence the bicylinder bears to the **cube** (:eq:`8r^3`) the exact ratio that the **sphere** bears to its circumscribed cylinder (:eq:`\frac{2}{3}`), proving that the bicylinder equals **two-thirds of the cube** (:eq:`V = \frac{16}{3}r^3`).
