=====
About
=====

Historical transmission, manuscript stemma, and archival provenance for ``geometor.archimedes``.

Three Streams of Textual Transmission
-------------------------------------

Our knowledge of :person:`Archimedes' <archimedes>` works descends through three extraordinary historical channels, all represented across the seven units of this codex:

1. **Archetype Codex A (Byzantine Minuscule, 9th c. → Renaissance)**: Compiled in ninth-century Constantinople in the school of :person:`Leo the Mathematician <leo-the-mathematician>`, translated word-for-word into Latin at Viterbo in 1269 by :person:`William of Moerbeke <moerbeke>` (Vatican *Ottob. lat. 1850*), copied by fifteenth-century Italian humanists before disappearing after 1564, and printed in the 1544 Basel *editio princeps* (:cite:`venatorius:1544`). Preserves *Sphere and Cylinder* I–II, *Measurement of a Circle*, *Conoids and Spheroids*, *On Spirals*, *Equilibrium of Planes* I–II, *The Sand-Reckoner*, *Quadrature of the Parabola*, and :person:`Eutocius's <eutocius>` Greek commentaries.
2. **The Arabic Tradition (*Kitāb al-Maʾkhūdhāt*)**: Ninth-century Baghdad geometer :person:`Thābit ibn Qurra <thabit-ibn-qurra>` translated the fifteen planar propositions of the *Book of Lemmas* into Arabic; :person:`Abraham Ecchellensis and Giovanni Alfonso Borelli <borelli>` published the Latin translation *Liber Assumptorum* at Florence in 1661 (:cite:`ecchellensis-borelli:1661`), preserving the *Arbelos*, *Twin Circles*, *Neusis Trisection*, and *Salinon*.
3. **Codex C — The Archimedes Palimpsest (10th c. → 1906 Discovery)**: A tenth-century Constantinople parchment scraped and overwritten in the thirteenth century as a Christian Euchologion, examined by :person:`Johan Ludvig Heiberg <heiberg>` in 1906 (:cite:`heiberg:1907`). Restored the Greek text of *On Floating Bodies*, uncovered fragments of the 14-piece *Stomachion*, and recovered the lost masterpiece **The Method of Mechanical Theorems Addressed to Eratosthenes**, translated into English in :person:`T. L. Heath's <heath>` 1912 Cambridge Supplement (:cite:`heath:1912`).

Public-Domain Archival Provenance
---------------------------------

All primary editions used in ``geometor.archimedes`` are 100% public-domain works whose metadata records are archived and SHA-256 checksummed in ``resources/works/sources_manifest.json``:

* **Heath (1897)**: *The Works of Archimedes*, Cambridge University Press (Internet Archive ``worksofarchimede00arch``).
* **Heath (1912)**: *The Method of Archimedes, Recently Discovered by Heiberg*, Cambridge University Press (Internet Archive ``methodofarchimed00archiala``).
* **Heiberg Vol. I (1880)**: *Archimedis Opera Omnia cum commentariis Eutocii*, Vol. I, B. G. Teubner, Leipzig (Internet Archive ``archimedisoperao01arch``).
* **Heiberg Vol. II (1881)**: *Archimedis Opera Omnia cum commentariis Eutocii*, Vol. II, B. G. Teubner, Leipzig (Internet Archive ``archimedisoperao02arch``).
