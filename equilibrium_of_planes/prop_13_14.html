Book I: Propositions 13 & 14: The Triangle Centroid X(2)¶

Proposition 13. In any triangle, the centre of gravity lies on the straight line joining any angle to the middle point of the opposite side.

Proposition 14. It follows at once that the centre of gravity of any triangle is at the intersection of the lines drawn from any two angles to the middle points of the opposite sides respectively.

Let ABC be a triangle, and D the middle point of BC. Join AD. If the centre of gravity H does not lie on AD, draw HIparallelBC meeting AD in I. Bisect DC, BD repeatedly and draw parallels to AD from the division points, inscribing in triangleABC a union of parallelograms symmetrical about AD such that the remaining small corner triangles (all similar to triangleABC) have a combined area having to the inscribed parallelograms a ratio less than (Eucl. X. 1, Eucl. VI. 19).

By Prop. 9, every inscribed parallelogram has its centre of gravity on AD; therefore the centre of gravity P of all the parallelograms taken together lies on AD. Let Q be the centre of gravity of the remaining corner triangles taken together; by Prop. 8, P, H, Q are collinear and:

\[\frac{\text{Remaining Triangles}}{\text{Inscribed Parallelograms}} = \frac{PH}{HQ}.\]

Draw KLparallelAD through Q meeting BC, AC; since all corner triangles lie between AD and its bounding tangents, Q cannot lie beyond the boundary parallel through C, implying , contradicting our construction! (Archimedes also supplies a pure similarity proof decomposing :g:`triangle ABC` into a central parallelogram :g:`FKEC` and two half-scale triangles :g:`triangle AFE`, :g:`triangle EBD` via :just:`Prop. 10` and :just:`Post. 5`.)

Applying Proposition 13 to two medians AD and BE yields Proposition 14: the three medians concur at the unique barycenter G dividing each median in the ratio (geometor:etc, Kimberling center X(2) with homogeneous barycentric coordinates ).
