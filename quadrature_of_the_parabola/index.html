Quadrature of the Parabola¶

Quadratura Parabolae — Archimedes of Syracuse (ed. T. L. Heath, 1897, pp. 233–252; J. L. Heiberg, Vol. II, pp. 294–353)

Note

Edition Track (English): Public-domain translation from heath:1897 (pp. 233–252), verified against 300 DPI scans of Heath (1897) and collated with Heiberg's critical Doric Greek edition (heiberg:1881, Vol. II, pp. 294–353). Switch tracks above for the Summary or Notes.

ΤΕΤΡΑΓΩΝΙΣΜΟΣ ΠΑΡΑΒΟΛΗΣ
ARCHIMEDIS TETRAGONISMOS PARABOLES

Author: Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BC)
Critical Greek Edition: Johan Ludvig Heiberg (Leipzig: Teubner, 1880–1881 / 1910–1915)
Public-Domain English Edition: Sir Thomas Little Heath (Cambridge University Press, 1897 & 1912)

“The excess by which the greater of (two) unequal areas exceeds the less can, by being added to itself, be made to exceed any given finite area.”

—Archimedes, Quadrature of the Parabola, Lemma of Archimedes

Proposition 9: Lever Bounds for a Right-Angled Trapezium Strip (II)

Proposition 11: Lever Bounds for an Oblique Trapezium Strip (II)

Proposition 11. Completes the case analysis for any trapezium bounded by two vertical parallels suspended from OB, proving that in all inclinations the balancing area at A lies strictly between the two endpoint-moment bounds.

Proposition 12: Balancing a Trapezium Cut from an Inclined Triangle (I)

Proposition 12. Applies Propositions 8–11 to a trapezium cut by parallels to the parabola diameter from the enveloping tangent triangle triangleQqT when Qq is perpendicular to the diameter, bounding the balancing weight at A via the intercept ratio of Proposition 4.

Proposition 13: Balancing a Trapezium Cut from an Inclined Triangle (II)

Proposition 13. Extends Proposition 12 to an arbitrary oblique chord Qq not perpendicular to the diameter, proving the corresponding lever inequalities for every trapezium strip cut from triangleQqT via Proposition 5.

Proposition 14: Inscribed & Circumscribed Trapezium Bounds (Perpendicular Base)

Proposition 14. Partitioning a right-angled base Qq of the parabolic segment into equal parts and drawing parallels to the diameter inscribes and circumscribes finite families of rectilinear trapezia inside triangleQqT. Summing the lever inequalities across all strips compresses the balancing weight of the parabolic segment between inscribed and circumscribed trapezium weights whose difference equals a single strip of width .

Proposition 15: Inscribed & Circumscribed Trapezium Bounds (Oblique Base)

Proposition 15. Extends Proposition 14 to any oblique chord Qq, showing that partitioning Qq into equal segments and summing the lever inequalities of Proposition 13 compresses the balancing weight of the parabolic segment within a single terminal triangle of base , which can be made smaller than any assigned area (Archimedes' Lemma).

Proposition 16: Mechanical Quadrature Against the Tangent Triangle

Proposition 16. By double indirect exhaustion on the balance beam (using Props. 14–15 and Archimedes' Lemma), the parabolic segment equals one-third of the enveloping tangent triangle triangleQqT.

Proposition 17: Area Equals Four-Thirds of Inscribed Triangle (Mechanical)

Proposition 17. The area of any segment of a parabola is four-thirds of the triangle which has the same base and equal height.

Proposition 18: Vertex & Diameter-Height of a Parabolic Segment

Proposition 18. If Qq be the base of a parabolic segment and V its midpoint, and if the diameter through V meet the parabola at P (the vertex where the tangent is parallel to Qq), then P is the point of greatest perpendicular distance from Qq, and the diameter through the midpoint of QV meets the tangent at P and the curve in points cutting off of PV along the diameter.

Proposition 19: Quarter-Height Scaling of Secondary Segments

Proposition 19. If diameters through the midpoints F, G of half-chords AB, BC (or AD) meet the parabola at secondary vertices D, E, then the diameter-segment height of each sub-segment is one-fourth of the main height ( measured along the diameter).

Proposition 20: Inscribed Triangle Exceeds Half the Parabolic Segment

Proposition 20. If ABC be a parabolic segment with vertex B, then triangleABC is greater than half the area of the segment ABC.

Proposition 21: Eightfold Triangle Ratio in Parabolic Subdivision

Proposition 21. If D, E be the vertices of the two remaining segments on AB and BC, then:

\[\triangle ABC = 8 \cdot \triangle ADB = 8 \cdot \triangle BEC, \qquad \text{whence} \qquad \triangle ADB + \triangle BEC = \frac{1}{4} \triangle ABC.\]

Proposition 22: Monotone Lower Bound of Inscribed Triangle Sums

Proposition 22. For any finite sequence of areas ABCDdots formed at successive stages of the parabolic triangle-tree bisection (, , etc.), their sum is strictly less than the area of the parabolic segment.

Proposition 23: Exact Finite Geometric Series Identity (1/4^n)

Proposition 23 (Exact Finite Geometric Series Identity). Given a series of areas ABCDdotsZ, of which A is the greatest, and each is equal to four times the next in order, then:

\[A + B + C + D + \dots + Z + \frac{1}{3} Z = \frac{4}{3} A.\]

Proposition 24: Pure Geometric Quadrature of the Parabola (4/3 ΔABC)

Proposition 24 (Geometric Quadrature of the Parabola). Every segment bounded by a parabola and a chord AC is equal to four-thirds of the triangle ABC which has the same base as the segment and equal height.
