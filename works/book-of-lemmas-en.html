Propositions 1–3: Tangent Circles, Semicircle Tangents & Chord Ratios¶

Propositions 4–6: The Arbelos (Ἄρβηλος) & Twin Circles¶

[Folio Heath (1897), pp. 304–309 · Heiberg II, 430–436 | p. 304]
Proposition 4 (Area of the Arbelos). If AB be the diameter of a semicircle and N any point on AB, and if semicircles be described within the first semicircle and having AN, NB as diameters respectively, the figure included between the circumferences of the three semicircles is ‘what Archimedes called ἄρβηλος’ (shoemaker’s knife); and its area is equal to the circle on PN as diameter, where PN is perpendicular to AB and meets the original semicircle in P.

Proof: By Eucl. III. 31 and Eucl. VI. 8, , so . Since circles and semicircles are to one another as the squares on their diameters (Eucl. XII. 2), the outer semicircle on AB equals the sum of the two inner semicircles on AN, NB together with twice the semicircle (i.e. one whole circle) on PN. Subtracting the two inner semicircles leaves the arbelos equal to the circle on PN!

Proposition 5 (Archimedes’ Twin Circles). Let AB be the diameter of a semicircle, C any point on AB, and CD perpendicular to it, and let semicircles be described within the first semicircle and having AC, CB as diameters. Then if two circles be drawn touching CD on different sides and each touching two of the semicircles, the circles so drawn will be equal (Archimedes' Twin Circles).

Proof: Let one circle touch CD at E, the outer semicircle AB at F, and the inner semicircle AC at G, and let its diameter EH be parallel to AB. By Proposition 1, FHG and BFA and AGC align homothetically, yielding ! That is:

\[AB \cdot EH = AC \cdot CB \implies EH = \frac{AC \cdot CB}{AB}.\]

Because the expression is symmetric in AC and CB, the second circle inscribed on the opposite side of CD (touching CD, semicircle AB, and semicircle CB) has diameter , strictly equal to EH!

Proposition 15 (Pentagon, Decagon & the Golden Section). Let AB be the diameter of a circle, AC a side of an inscribed regular pentagon, D the middle point of the arc AC. Join CD and produce it to meet BA produced in E; join AC, DB meeting in F, and draw FM perpendicular to AB. Then EM is equal to the radius of the circle (and EC is cut in extreme and mean ratio , Eucl. XIII. 9!).
