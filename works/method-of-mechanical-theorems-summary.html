The Method of Mechanical Theorems — Analytical Summary¶

Scope: Heiberg (1907 / Vol. II², pp. 426–507) · Heath Supplement (1912), pp. 12–51 · Epistle to Eratosthenes, Lemmas 1–11 & Propositions 1–15

Unveiling the Workshop of Antiquity’s Greatest Geometer¶

For over two millennia, Archimedes' published treatises inspired awe and perplexity: how did he guess exact ratios like , , , or before setting up his intricate double-reductio exhaustion proofs? Deciphered by J. L. Heiberg in Constantinople in 1906 from the tenth-century Palimpsest (Codex C), The Method (archimedes:methodus) reveals Archimedes’ private analytic engine:

  1. Cavalieri Cross-Sections + Virtual Lever (:term:`ἔφοδος <ephodion>`): Decompose an unknown area or solid X and a known comparison figure Y into one-parameter families of lower-dimensional slices along axis . Using conic/quadric symptomata, construct a virtual lever arm H beyond the fulcrum A (HA = a or 2r) so that . Moving every slice to H concentrates X at a single point, balancing Y at its known centroid :

    \[a \cdot \text{Vol}(X) = \bar{x}_Y \cdot \text{Vol}(Y).\]

  2. Methodological Separation of Discovery and Proof: Archimedes draws a razor-sharp philosophical boundary between indication (ἔνδειξις, summing unextended planar/linear indivisibles on a mechanical lever) and synthetic demonstration (ἀπόδειξις, rigorous Eudoxean exhaustion by finite magnitudes).
