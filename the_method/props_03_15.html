Propositions 3–15: Conoids, Centroids, the Ungula & the Bicylinder¶

  • Propositions 3–11 (pp. 21–38): Apply the identical slice-lever engine to discover the volumes of spheroids (Prop. 3), paraboloids (Prop. 4), centroids of paraboloid segments ( along the axis, Prop. 5!), centroids of hemispheres ( from pole to centre, i.e. from the base, Prop. 6!), spherical segments (Props. 7, 9), spheroid segments (Props. 8, 10), and hyperboloid segments & centroids (Prop. 11).

  • Propositions 12–14: The Cylindrical Ungula (pp. 38–48): First promised in the Preface to Eratosthenes, Archimedes proves three times—first mechanically by balancing vertical rectangular slices against a prism (Props. 12–13), then geometrically via parabolic triangles and exhaustion by inscribed/circumscribed prism elements (Prop. 14)—that the cylindrical ungula cut from a right cylinder in a square-based prism equals one-sixth of the whole prism (), a curved solid equal to a purely rectilinear fraction of its bounding box without !

  • Proposition 15: The Archimedean Bicylinder (pp. 48–51): Slicing two orthogonal intersecting cylinders inscribed in a cube of edge by planes parallel to both axes produces square cross-sections of area —identical in ratio at every height to the circular cross-sections of the inscribed sphere! Hence the bicylinder bears to the cube () the exact ratio that the sphere bears to its circumscribed cylinder (), proving that the bicylinder equals two-thirds of the cube ().
