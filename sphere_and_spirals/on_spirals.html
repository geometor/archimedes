On Spirals: Kinematic Definition, Subtangent Rectification & Quadrature¶

Kinematic Definition & Discrete Sum of Squares¶

  • Definition 1 (Heath p. 154): If a straight line drawn in a plane revolve at a uniform rate about one extremity which remains fixed, and return to the position from which it started, and if, at the same time as the line revolves, a point move at a uniform rate along the straight line beginning from the extremity which remains fixed, the point will describe a spiral (ἕλιξ) in the plane ().

  • Proposition 10 (Discrete Integral of Squares): Given lines in arithmetic progression (), Archimedes proves the exact double inequality:

    \[3 \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} A_k^2 + A_n^2 + d\sum_{k=1}^{n} A_k > (n + 1)\text{-fold identity} \implies 3\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} k^2 < n^3 < 3\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2,\]

Propositions 18–20 (Subtangent Rectification) & Proposition 24 (Quadrature)¶

f. Heath (1897), pp. 171–182 · Heiberg II, 72–115 · p. 171

  • Proposition 18 (Differential Subtangent Theorem):

    If OA be the initial line, A the extremity of the first turn of the spiral, and if the tangent to the spiral at A be drawn meeting the perpendicular OT to OA at T, then the polar subtangent OT is equal to the circumference () of the ‘first circle’ having radius OA.

    Proved by double indirect kinematic neusis bounds (Props. 5–9): assuming or constructs a chord/secant contradicting the uniform ratio of angular arc to radial growth ()! Props. 19–20 generalize to the -th turn and arbitrary intermediate angles.

  • Proposition 24 (Integral Quadrature of the Spiral):

    The area bounded by the first turn of the spiral and the initial line OA is equal to one-third of the ‘first circle’ ().

    Dividing the circle into equal sectors inscribes and circumscribes circular sectors of radii (), squeezing the spiral area via Proposition 10 ()! Subsequent turns satisfy and general ring ratios (Props. 25–28).
