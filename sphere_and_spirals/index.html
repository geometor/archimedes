“Every cylinder whose base is the greatest circle in a sphere and whose height is equal to the diameter of the sphere is once and a half as great as the sphere, and its surface together with its bases is likewise once and a half the surface of the sphere.”

—Archimedes, On the Sphere and Cylinder I, Corollary to Proposition 34 (The Tomb Theorem)

Sphere and Cylinder, On Spirals & Conoids — Philological & Mathematical Notes

In Tusculan Disputations V.23, Cicero records how, serving as quaestor in Sicily in 75 BC, he cleared away brambles outside the Agrigentine Gate of Syracuse and rediscovered the forgotten tomb of Archimedes by recognizing on a small column the sphere and circumscribed cylinder of Sphere and Cylinder I.34 Corollary, accompanied by worn Greek iambic verses declaring that the cylinder’s volume and surface stood in the sesquialter ratio to the sphere. As The Method, Prop. 2 reveals in Archimedes’ own words, he discovered the volume first by weighing slices on a virtual balance, and then conjectured by analogy with Measurement of a Circle Prop. 1 ()!
