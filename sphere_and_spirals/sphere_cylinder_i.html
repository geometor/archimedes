On the Sphere and Cylinder I: Assumptions 1–5 & The Tomb Theorem¶

Definitions & Five Convexity / Continuity Assumptions¶

Addressing Dositheus after mourning Conon (archimedes:sphere-and-cylinder), Archimedes defines lines and surfaces concave in the same direction (ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ κοῖλα, Defs. 2 & 4)—meaning that the straight segment joining any two points on the curve or surface falls either wholly on the same side of, or upon, the curve or surface—and lays down five foundational Assumptions (Λαμβανόμενα) (Heath pp. 3–4):

  1. Assumption 1: Of all lines which have the same extremities, the straight line is the least. (Explicitly cited by Proclus, proclus:in-euclidem, Friedlein p. 110.)

  2. Assumption 2: Of other plane lines having the same extremities and concave in the same direction, when one is wholly included between the other and the straight line having the same extremities (or partially coincides), that which is included is the lesser.

  3. Assumption 3: Similarly, of surfaces having the same plane extremities, the plane is the least in area.

  4. Assumption 4: Of other surfaces having the same plane extremities and concave in the same direction, when one is wholly included by the other, the included surface is the lesser.

  5. Assumption 5 (Archimedes' Lemma): Further, of unequal lines, unequal surfaces, and unequal solids, the greater exceeds the less by such a magnitude as, when added to itself, can be made to exceed any assigned magnitude among those which are comparable with one another.

Book I, Propositions 33 & 34: Surface, Volume & the Tomb Theorem¶

f. Heath (1897), pp. 39–44 · Heiberg I, 134–149 · p. 39

Inscribing and circumscribing regular -gons to a great circle of the sphere and revolving them about a diameter generates solids bounded by conical frusta whose lateral areas sum (via Props. 21–32) to times a circle of radius approaching . Double exhaustion yields the two crowning theorems of Book I:

Proposition 33. The surface of any sphere is equal to four times its greatest circle ().

Proposition 34. Any sphere is equal to four times the cone which has its base equal to the greatest circle in the sphere and its height equal to the radius of the sphere ().

Corollary (The Tomb Engraving). From what has been proved it follows that every cylinder whose base is the greatest circle in a sphere and whose height is equal to the diameter of the sphere is once and a half () as great as the sphere, and its surface together with its bases is likewise once and a half () the surface of the sphere.

In Props. 42–43, Archimedes further proves that the curved surface of any spherical segment (less than or greater than a hemisphere) equals the circle whose radius is the straight line drawn from the vertex of the segment to the circumference of its base circle!
