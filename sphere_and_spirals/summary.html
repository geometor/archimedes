Sphere and Cylinder, On Spirals & Conoids — Analytical Summary¶

Scope: Heiberg Vol. I, pp. 1–255, 272–499; Vol. II, pp. 1–141 · Heath (1897), pp. 1–90, 99–188

Master Synthesis of Curved Surfaces, Solids & Kinematic Loci¶

Across these three treatises addressed to Dositheus, Archimedes builds the complete pre-Newtonian integral and differential calculus of circles, spheres, quadrics of revolution, and polar spirals:

  1. Convexity & Arc/Surface Length Foundations (*Sphere & Cylinder* I, Assump. 1–5): Because neither Euclid nor Eudoxus possessed an axiom comparing curved arc lengths or curved surface areas against rectilinear bounds, Archimedes formulates ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ κοῖλα (convexity) so that nested convex perimeters and surfaces obey strict monotonicity.

  2. Discrete Power Sums as Definite Integrals: - Linear sum Paraboloid segment cylinder cone (Conoids Props. 19–22). - Quadratic sum Spiral sector circle (On Spirals Props. 10, 24), Hyperboloid (Conoids Props. 25–26), and Spheroid (Conoids Props. 27–32).
