Proposition 1: Circle Area Equal to Right-Angled Triangle

The area of any circle is equal to a right-angled triangle in which one of the sides about the right angle is equal to the radius, and the other to the circumference, of the circle.

Proposition 1: Homothetic Chords of Tangent Circles

Proposition 1 (Homothetic Chords of Tangent Circles). If two circles touch at A, and if CD, EF be parallel diameters in them, then ADF is a straight line (and similarly ACE when tangent externally).

Book I, Proposition 1: Equal Weights at Equal Distances

Proposition 1. Weights which balance at equal distances are equal.

Proposition 3. Unequal weights will balance at unequal distances, the greater weight being at the lesser distance (and conversely, if two unequal weights balance, the greater weight is at the lesser distance).

Proposition 3: Ordinate-Squared Symptomata of the Parabola

Proposition 3. If from a point on a parabola a straight line PV be drawn parallel to the axis, and from two other points Q, Q on the parabola straight lines QV, QV be drawn parallel to the tangent at P meeting PV in V, V, then:

Proposition 3: Volume of a Spheroid (Ellipsoid of Revolution)

Proposition 3. Any spheroid is four times the cone which has its base equal to the greatest circle of the spheroid (perpendicular to the axis of revolution) and its height equal to half the axis; and the cylinder having the same base and height equal to the whole axis is times the spheroid.

Proposition 4: Area of the Arbelos (Ἄρβηλος)

Proposition 4 (Area of the Arbelos). If AB be the diameter of a semicircle and N any point on AB, and if semicircles be described within the first semicircle and having AN, NB as diameters respectively, the figure included between the circumferences of the three semicircles is ‘what Archimedes called ἄρβηλος’ (shoemaker’s knife); and its area is equal to the circle on PN as diameter, where PN is perpendicular to AB and meets the original semicircle in P.

Book I, Proposition 4: Centre of Gravity of Two Equal Magnitudes

Proposition 4. If two equal magnitudes have not the same centre of gravity, the centre of gravity of both taken together is at the middle point of the straight line joining their centres of gravity.

Proposition 4: Intercept Ratio Along Parabola Diameter & Tangent (I)

Proposition 4: Volume of a Paraboloid of Revolution

Proposition 4. Any segment of a right-angled conoid (paraboloid of revolution) cut off by a plane at right angles to the axis is :eq:`1frac{1}{2}` times the cone which has the same base as the segment and the same axis.

Proposition 5: Archimedes’ Twin Circles

Proposition 5 (Archimedes’ Twin Circles). Let AB be the diameter of a semicircle, C any point on AB, and CD perpendicular to it, and let semicircles be described within the first semicircle and having AC, CB as diameters. Then if two circles be drawn touching CD on different sides and each touching two of the semicircles, the circles so drawn will be equal (Archimedes' Twin Circles).

Book I, Proposition 5: Centre of Gravity of Equally Spaced Equal Magnitudes

Proposition 5 (with Corollaries 1–2). If three equal magnitudes have their centres of gravity on a straight line at equal distances, the centre of gravity of the system will coincide with that of the middle magnitude. (Corollaries 1–2: The same holds for any odd number of equally spaced equal magnitudes; for any even number, it lies at the midpoint between the two middle magnitudes.)

Proposition 5: Centre of Gravity of a Paraboloid Segment

Proposition 5. The centre of gravity of a segment of a right-angled conoid (paraboloid of revolution) cut off by a plane at right angles to the axis lies on the axis, dividing it so that the portion adjacent to the vertex is double the remaining portion adjacent to the base ( from vertex to base).

Proposition 5: Intercept Ratio Along Parabola Diameter & Tangent (II)

Proposition 5. If Qq be the base of any segment of a parabola, P the vertex of the segment, and PV its diameter, and if the diameter of the parabola through any other point R meet Qq in O, the curve in R, and the tangent at q (or Q) in E, then for any oblique chord Qq:

\[QO : Oq = ER : RO.\]

Book I, Proposition 6: Law of the Lever for Commensurable Magnitudes

Proposition 6. Commensurable magnitudes balance at distances reciprocally proportional to the weights.

Proposition 6: Centre of Gravity of a Hemisphere

Proposition 6. The centre of gravity of any hemisphere lies on its axis, dividing the radius in the ratio from the vertex (pole) to the centre of the sphere (i.e. at distance from the base).

Proposition 7: Circumscribed & Inscribed Squares of a Circle

Proposition 7: If circles be circumscribed about and inscribed in a square, the circumscribed circle is double of the inscribed circle ().

Book I, Proposition 7: Law of the Lever for Incommensurable Magnitudes

Proposition 7. And even if the magnitudes are incommensurable, they will likewise balance at distances reciprocally proportional to the magnitudes (archimedes:equilibrium-of-planes:i-7).

Proposition 7: Balancing an Obtuse- or Acute-Angled Triangle on a Lever

Proposition 7: Volume of a Segment of a Sphere

Proposition 7. Any segment of a sphere has to the cone which has the same base as the segment and the same height the ratio which the sum of the radius of the sphere and the height of the complementary segment has to the height of the complementary segment (matching Sphere and Cylinder II.2).

Proposition 8: Neusis Angle Trisection

Proposition 8 (Neusis Trisection of the Angle). If AB be any chord of a circle whose centre is O, and if AB be produced to C so that BC is equal to the radius (neusis insertion meeting the diameter DOE produced at C), and if CO meet the circle in D and be produced to meet the circle a second time in E, the arc AE will be equal to three times the arc BD.

Book I, Proposition 8: Centre of Gravity of a Remainder Magnitude

Proposition 8. If AB be a magnitude whose centre of gravity is C, and AD a part of it whose centre of gravity is F, then the centre of gravity G of the remaining part DE lies on FC produced so that .

Proposition 8: Lever Bounds for a Right-Angled Trapezium Strip (I)

Proposition 8. If a triangle BDC suspended from OB (with ) be cut by a vertical line into a smaller triangle and a trapezium FDEC lying between verticals through E and C, and if an area P hung at A balances the trapezium in situ, then:

\[OA : OE > \text{Trapezium } FDEC : P > OA : OC.\]

Proposition 8: Volume of a Segment of a Spheroid

Proposition 8. Any segment of a spheroid cut off by a plane at right angles to the axis bears to the cone with the same base and height the ratio of half the axis plus the height of the complementary segment to the height of the complementary segment (extending Prop. 7 to ellipsoids of revolution).

Proposition 9: Intersecting Chords Arc-Sum Lemma

Proposition 9 (Intersecting Chords Arc-Sum Lemma). If in a circle two chords AB, CD which do not pass through the centre intersect at right angles, then the sum of the opposite arcs ADBC is equal to the sum of the remaining opposite arcs ACBD (each pair equals a semicircle).

Book I, Proposition 9: Parallelogram Centre of Gravity on Bimedians

Proposition 9. In any parallelogram (Παντὸς παραλληλογράμμου τὸ κέντρον τοῦ βάρεός ἐστιν…, Heiberg II, 162), the centre of gravity lies on the straight line joining the middle points of opposite sides.

Proposition 10: Secant & Tangent Segments from an External Point

Proposition 10 (Secant & Tangent Segments from an External Point). Suppose a circle touches two given lines or is cut by secants from an external point T, with tangent chord AB and secant chords joining points of contact—establishing chord-tangent segment proportions later connected to Apollonius’s On Contacts (Tangencies).

Proposition 9: Centre of Gravity of a Segment of a Sphere

Proposition 9. The centre of gravity of any segment of a sphere (of height on a sphere of radius ) lies on its axis, dividing from vertex to base in the ratio — i.e., at distance from the base.

Proposition 9: Lever Bounds for a Right-Angled Trapezium Strip (II)

Proposition 9. Extends the lever inequality of Proposition 8 to an arbitrary vertical trapezium suspended between E and C on the beam OB when the upper base is inclined rather than horizontal, bounding its balancing weight P at A strictly between the moments at the two vertical boundaries E and C.

Book I, Proposition 10: Parallelogram Centre of Gravity at Diagonal Intersection

Proposition 10 (with Alternative Proof). In any parallelogram, the centre of gravity is the point of intersection of its diagonals.

Proposition 10: Centre of Gravity of a Segment of a Spheroid

Proposition 10. The centre of gravity of any segment of a spheroid cut off by a plane perpendicular to the axis divides the axis of the segment in the identical algebraic ratio to the corresponding spherical segment of Proposition 9.

Proposition 10: Lever Bounds for an Oblique Trapezium Strip (I)

Proposition 10. If a trapezium suspended beneath OB has parallel vertical sides through E and C and oblique non-parallel sides meeting at a point on the opposite side of the vertical, its balancing weight P at A satisfies the analogous two-sided lever inequality between OE and OC (Equil. I. 15).

Proposition 11: Orthogonal Chords Quadrance Invariant

Proposition 11 (Orthogonal Chords Quadrance Invariant). If two chords AB, CD in a circle intersect at right angles in a point O, not being the centre, then:

\[AO^2 + BO^2 + CO^2 + DO^2 = (\text{diameter})^2 = 4 R^2.\]

Book I, Proposition 11: Centres of Gravity in Similar Triangles (I)

Proposition 11. If two triangles be similar, and two points within them be similarly situated with respect to the triangles, and if one point is the centre of gravity of the first triangle, the other point is likewise the centre of gravity of the second triangle.

Proposition 11: Hyperboloid Segment Volume & Centre of Gravity

Proposition 11. Determines by the mechanical slice-lever method both the volume ratio (to the inscribed cone) and the centre of gravity along the axis of any segment of an obtuse-angled conoid (two-sheeted hyperboloid of revolution), matching the results later proved by exhaustion in On Conoids and Spheroids.

Proposition 11: Lever Bounds for an Oblique Trapezium Strip (II)

Proposition 11. Completes the case analysis for any trapezium bounded by two vertical parallels suspended from OB, proving that in all inclinations the balancing area at A lies strictly between the two endpoint-moment bounds.

Proposition 12: Polar-Orthocenter Property of Semicircle Tangents

Proposition 12 (Polar-Orthocenter Property of Semicircle Tangents). If AB be the diameter of a semicircle, and TP, TQ the tangents to it from any point T, and if AQ, BP be joined meeting in R, then TR produced is perpendicular to AB.

Book I, Proposition 12: Centres of Gravity in Similar Triangles (II)

Proposition 12. If two triangles be similar, and the centre of gravity of one of them lie on the straight line joining an angle to the middle point of the opposite side, the centre of gravity of the other will also lie on the straight line similarly drawn in it.

Proposition 12: Balancing a Trapezium Cut from an Inclined Triangle (I)

Proposition 12. Applies Propositions 8–11 to a trapezium cut by parallels to the parabola diameter from the enveloping tangent triangle triangleQqT when Qq is perpendicular to the diameter, bounding the balancing weight at A via the intercept ratio of Proposition 4.

Proposition 12: Mechanical Slice Discovery of the Cylindrical Ungula

Proposition 12 (Cylindrical Ungula — Mechanical Method). If in a right prism with square base a cylinder be inscribed with its bases in opposite squares and its curved surface touching the four lateral faces, and a plane be drawn through one side of the square base and the centre of the opposite square, the cylindrical ungula (hoof) cut off is one-sixth of the whole prism ().

Proposition 13: Equal Chord Segments Cut by Diameter Perpendiculars

Proposition 13 (Equal Chord Segments Cut by Diameter Perpendiculars). If a diameter AB of a circle meet any chord CD, not a diameter, in E, and if AM, BN be the perpendiculars let fall from A, B upon CD, then .

Book I, Proposition 13: Triangle Centre of Gravity Lies on Each Median

Proposition 13. In any triangle, the centre of gravity lies on the straight line joining any angle to the middle point of the opposite side.

Proposition 13: Alternative Slice Balance for the Cylindrical Ungula

Proposition 13. Establishes the prism-to-ungula ratio a second time via parabolic-section chords and vertical prism slices balanced in situ against a triangular prism, preparing the strict geometric exhaustion of Proposition 14.

Proposition 13: Balancing a Trapezium Cut from an Inclined Triangle (II)

Proposition 13. Extends Proposition 12 to an arbitrary oblique chord Qq not perpendicular to the diameter, proving the corresponding lever inequalities for every trapezium strip cut from triangleQqT via Proposition 5.

Proposition 14: The Salinon (‘Salt-Cellar’)

Proposition 14 (The Salinon / ‘Salt-Cellar’). Let ACB be a semicircle on AB as diameter, and let AD, BE be equal lengths measured along AB from A, B respectively. On AD, BE as diameters describe semicircles on the side towards C, and on DE as diameter a semicircle on the opposite side. Let the perpendicular to AB through O, the centre of the first semicircle, meet the opposite semicircles in C, F respectively. Then shall the area of the figure bounded by the circumferences of all the semicircles (‘which Archimedes calls a σάλινον’) be equal to the area of the circle whose diameter is CF.

Book I, Proposition 14: Concurrency of Medians at Triangle Centroid X(2)

Proposition 14. It follows at once that the centre of gravity of any triangle is at the intersection of the lines drawn from any two angles to the middle points of the opposite sides respectively.

Proposition 14: Inscribed & Circumscribed Trapezium Bounds (Perpendicular Base)

Proposition 14. Partitioning a right-angled base Qq of the parabolic segment into equal parts and drawing parallels to the diameter inscribes and circumscribes finite families of rectilinear trapezia inside triangleQqT. Summing the lever inequalities across all strips compresses the balancing weight of the parabolic segment between inscribed and circumscribed trapezium weights whose difference equals a single strip of width .

Proposition 14: Pure Geometric Exhaustion of the Cylindrical Ungula

Proposition 14. Strictly demonstrates geometrically—without mechanical postulates, using inscribed and circumscribed prism elements bounded by parabolic arcs and double indirect exhaustion—that the cylindrical ungula is one-sixth of the circumscribing square prism.

Proposition 15: Pentagon, Decagon & the Golden Section

Proposition 15 (Pentagon, Decagon & the Golden Section). Let AB be the diameter of a circle, AC a side of an inscribed regular pentagon, D the middle point of the arc AC. Join CD and produce it to meet BA produced in E; join AC, DB meeting in F, and draw FM perpendicular to AB. Then EM is equal to the radius of the circle (and EC is cut in extreme and mean ratio , Eucl. XIII. 9!).

Book I: Proposition 15: Centre of Gravity of a Trapezium

Proposition 15. In any trapezium having two sides parallel to one another, the centre of gravity lies on the straight line joining the middle points of the parallel sides, at the point dividing it so that the segment adjacent to the smaller parallel side is to the remaining segment as twice the greater parallel side together with the smaller is to twice the smaller together with the greater:

\[GE : GF = (2 \cdot BC + AD) : (2 \cdot AD + BC).\]

Proposition 15: Inscribed & Circumscribed Trapezium Bounds (Oblique Base)

Proposition 15. Extends Proposition 14 to any oblique chord Qq, showing that partitioning Qq into equal segments and summing the lever inequalities of Proposition 13 compresses the balancing weight of the parabolic segment within a single terminal triangle of base , which can be made smaller than any assigned area (Archimedes' Lemma).

Proposition 15: Mechanical & Geometric Cubature of the Bicylinder

Proposition 15 (The Archimedean Bicylinder). If two cylinders of equal diameter be inscribed at right angles to one another within a cube of edge , the solid common to both cylinders (bicylinder / groin vault) is equal to two-thirds of the cube ().

Book II, Proposition 1: Equilibrium of Equal or Unequal Parabolic Segments

Proposition 1. If two parabolic segments have equal areas, or if their areas have a given ratio, they balance at equal distances or at distances reciprocally proportional to their areas as plane laminae (Book I, Props. 6–7).

Proposition 16: Mechanical Quadrature Against the Tangent Triangle

Proposition 16. By double indirect exhaustion on the balance beam (using Props. 14–15 and Archimedes' Lemma), the parabolic segment equals one-third of the enveloping tangent triangle triangleQqT.

Book II, Proposition 2: Centre of Gravity of Parabolic Segment Lies on Diameter

Proposition 2. If a triangle triangleABC be inscribed in a parabolic segment in the known manner (with base BC and vertex A), and two further triangles triangleADB, triangleBEC be inscribed in the remaining segments, and so on recursively, the centre of gravity of the whole segment lies on its diameter AO.

Proposition 17: Area Equals Four-Thirds of Inscribed Triangle (Mechanical)

Proposition 17. The area of any segment of a parabola is four-thirds of the triangle which has the same base and equal height.

Book II, Proposition 3: Exhaustion Polygons of Equal-Base Parabolic Segments

Proposition 3. If two parabolic segments have equal bases and equal heights, and rectilinear figures be inscribed in them in the known manner with the same number of sides, the centres of gravity of the two inscribed figures divide their respective diameters in the same ratio.

Proposition 18: Vertex & Diameter-Height of a Parabolic Segment

Proposition 18. If Qq be the base of a parabolic segment and V its midpoint, and if the diameter through V meet the parabola at P (the vertex where the tangent is parallel to Qq), then P is the point of greatest perpendicular distance from Qq, and the diameter through the midpoint of QV meets the tangent at P and the curve in points cutting off of PV along the diameter.

Book II, Proposition 4: Diameter-Division Invariance of Inscribed Polygons

Proposition 4. The centre of gravity of any inscribed exhaustion polygon in a parabolic segment lies on the diameter and approaches the segment’s centre of gravity as the number of subdivisions increases.

Proposition 19: Quarter-Height Scaling of Secondary Segments

Proposition 19. If diameters through the midpoints F, G of half-chords AB, BC (or AD) meet the parabola at secondary vertices D, E, then the diameter-segment height of each sub-segment is one-fourth of the main height ( measured along the diameter).

Book II, Proposition 5: Inscribed Polygon Centroid Closer to Base than Segment

Proposition 5. If a rectilinear figure be inscribed in a parabolic segment in the known manner, the centre of gravity of the whole segment is nearer to the vertex of the segment than is the centre of gravity of the inscribed figure.

Proposition 20: Inscribed Triangle Exceeds Half the Parabolic Segment

Proposition 20. If ABC be a parabolic segment with vertex B, then triangleABC is greater than half the area of the segment ABC.

Book II, Proposition 6: Bounded Approach of Inscribed Centroids

Proposition 6. Given a parabolic segment, it is possible to inscribe in it a rectilinear figure in the known manner such that the distance between the centres of gravity of the segment and of the inscribed figure is less than any assigned length.

Proposition 21: Eightfold Triangle Ratio in Parabolic Subdivision

Proposition 21. If D, E be the vertices of the two remaining segments on AB and BC, then:

\[\triangle ABC = 8 \cdot \triangle ADB = 8 \cdot \triangle BEC, \qquad \text{whence} \qquad \triangle ADB + \triangle BEC = \frac{1}{4} \triangle ABC.\]

Book II, Proposition 7: Constant Diameter Ratio Across All Parabolic Segments

Proposition 7. If two parabolic segments have their centres of gravity on their respective diameters, the centres of gravity divide the two diameters in the same ratio across all parabolic segments (whether right or oblique).

Proposition 22. For any finite sequence of areas ABCDdots formed at successive stages of the parabolic triangle-tree bisection (, , etc.), their sum is strictly less than the area of the parabolic segment.

Proposition 23: Exact Finite Geometric Series Identity (1/4^n)

Proposition 23 (Exact Finite Geometric Series Identity). Given a series of areas ABCDdotsZ, of which A is the greatest, and each is equal to four times the next in order, then:

Proposition 24: Pure Geometric Quadrature of the Parabola (4/3 ΔABC)

Book II, Proposition 10: Centre of Gravity of a Parabolic Frustum
