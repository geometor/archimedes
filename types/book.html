Measurement of a Circle

“The area of any circle is equal to a right-angled triangle in which one of the sides about the right angle is equal to the radius, and the other to the circumference, of the circle.”

—Archimedes, Measurement of a Circle, Proposition 1

Quadrature of the Parabola

“The excess by which the greater of (two) unequal areas exceeds the less can, by being added to itself, be made to exceed any given finite area.”

—Archimedes, Quadrature of the Parabola, Lemma of Archimedes

“Commensurable magnitudes balance at distances reciprocally proportional to the weights.”

—Archimedes, On the Equilibrium of Planes, Book I, Proposition 6

Sphere and Cylinder, Spirals & Conoids

“Every cylinder whose base is the greatest circle in a sphere and whose height is equal to the diameter of the sphere is once and a half as great as the sphere, and its surface together with its bases is likewise once and a half the surface of the sphere.”

—Archimedes, On the Sphere and Cylinder I, Corollary to Proposition 34 (The Tomb Theorem)

The Method of Mechanical Theorems

“For certain things first became clear to me by a mechanical method, although they had to be demonstrated by geometry afterwards because their investigation by the said method did not furnish an actual demonstration.”

—Archimedes, The Method Addressed to Eratosthenes

Book of Lemmas

“The figure included between the circumferences of the three semicircles is what Archimedes called ἄρβηλος (shoemaker’s knife); and its area is equal to the circle on PN as diameter.”

—Archimedes, Book of Lemmas, Proposition 4

The Sand-Reckoner & Semi-Regular Solids

“There are some, king Gelon, who think that the number of the sand is infinite in multitude… His hypotheses are that the fixed stars and the sun remain unmoved, that the earth revolves about the sun in the circumference of a circle, the sun lying in the middle of the orbit.”

—Archimedes, The Sand-Reckoner
